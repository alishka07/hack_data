# User-provided workplace policy: food as personal belongings

Policy revision recorded: 2026-10-18. Current application reference: `/policies/workplace-belongings`. The legacy `/policies/workplace-eating` URL remains an alias to this policy history.

The user explicitly revised the workplace rule: food on the cashier work table is treated as personal belongings at the workplace and is a violation. Visible eating is no longer required. This record documents the user's application policy; it does not claim an independently verified employer document, public-law provision or statutory citation.

The application applies rule `personal_food_at_workstation` when source-referenced video evidence clearly identifies food or a snack placed on an operating cashier work table, with visible working context. Food presence is sufficient. Eating, chewing, hand-to-mouth motion, a consumption sequence and inferred personal ownership are not prerequisites. The result identifies this as personal belongings at the workplace under the user-provided policy and includes the observed timestamp and evidence frames.

The object must actually be identifiable as food. An ambiguous package, bag, cup with unknown contents, empty plate or wrapper, medicine, medical device, cash or work equipment alone is insufficient. Food outside the cashier work surface, in a designated break area, or with an unresolved nonworking context does not establish this rule. The application does not infer shift schedules from appearance. Unreadable or incomplete context remains unknown.

This revision does not broaden the separate public-clause review of bags, phones, medicines, medical devices or qualifying communications devices. Those items retain their prior scope, evidence requirements and exceptions.

Supported findings include source timestamps and evidence JPEGs and remain subject to human review. Model confidence is uncalibrated; this policy does not claim measured detection accuracy.
