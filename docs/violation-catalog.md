# Cash-discipline candidate catalog

Current inference pipeline: `5.0`, continuous scoring `2.1`. The catalog has 33 entries: the original 32 reviewed items plus an explicitly unmapped vault-storage review concern. Nine curated visual scenarios now receive candidate screening and source-grounded verification, including all seven category names hinted in the simplified workbook. The separate three automatic legal gates retain their applicability and exception requirements. See [architecture 5.0](architecture-v5.md) and [label coverage](label-coverage-v5.md). The detailed scoring discussion below originated with the earlier catalog implementation.

The catalog has 6 event entries starting at **0%** and 27 required-procedure entries starting at **100%**. Scores change continuously with source-grounded model confidence:

| Check kind | Update within one episode | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event occurrence | `score = max(previous score, 100 × occurrence confidence)` | 42% confidence raises 0% to 42%; stronger 81% evidence raises it to 81%. |
| Required procedure | `score = min(previous score, 100 × (1 − correct-completion confidence))` | 65% confidence in correct completion lowers 100% to 35%; stronger 91% evidence lowers it to 9%. |

Confidence refers to support for the specified event or **correct completion of the applicable protocol**. Partial evidence may move that estimate without establishing completed performance. Unknown conditions lower confidence without automatically forcing every estimate to zero. A generic counting action cannot clear an obligation requiring the right cash, client presence and completion before handover. Model self-confidence is **uncalibrated**; a displayed 81% is an estimate, not an empirically measured 81% chance of a legal violation. Initial 0% does not prove absence, and initial 100% means completion has not been established, rather than an observed omission. No initial value or score alone creates a confirmed finding.

**Assessment coverage:** 11 visible checks—the 3 automatic event checks and all 8 review-only procedures—are assessed in the existing window model calls, without additional model calls for scoring. Their source-linked estimates update before the separate legal gate. An observable supported action can change the estimated score while legal applicability remains unknown; only the legal gate can confirm an automatic finding. Source-linked uncertain nonzero estimates are retained as `partial_support`, with the original model state recorded separately. Missing/occluded evidence leaves the prior score unchanged. The other 21 checks require manual or external-record verification and retain their initial score until that evidence is supplied. No face recognition, real-world identification, personal ownership, authorization or hidden-system conclusions may be inferred.

**Episode scope:** host-ledger observed start cues create fresh assessments for new transactions. When boundaries are not established, windows stay isolated; a completion observed in one window cannot clear another transaction. Within one episode, stronger support moves scores in the requested direction, and repeated or overlapping frames never add confidence. The summary shows the highest remaining estimate across relevant assessed episodes, so a completed procedure in one episode cannot hide another pending episode. Applicability, visibility and evidence status remain separate; positively evidenced N/A is excluded. These unlike scores are never added or averaged.

Known transaction episodes missing a check's model assessment are explicitly marked incomplete coverage. The summary concerns assessed episodes and cannot certify unassessed ones. Window-only observations retain an unconfirmed-boundary warning. An unknown-applicability window with zero support and no established transaction boundary records a coverage gap, instead of creating a new 100% pending procedure that masks another window's supported estimate.

The two mirrored pairs are `cash_intake_without_client_presence` / `intake_client_presence_required` and `previous_transaction_cash_exposed` / `previous_cash_stored`. Their `related_ids` are explicit in the JSON. Treat each pair as two views of the same duty; do not sum them as independent breaches.

## Source status and limits

The original 32 items retain **`prior_primary_review`**; the new `vault_cash_segregation_storage` item is **`unmapped_review_only`**, with no fabricated clause or URL. The underlying public-clause review is recorded in [local legal research notes](../tmp/planning/legal/verified_rules.md) and [Resolution 231 notes](../rules/sources/resolution-231.md) / [Resolution 14 notes](../rules/sources/resolution-14.md). The official consolidated sources are [NBK Resolution 231 of 29 November 2019](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V1900019680) and [NBK Resolution 14 of 24 February 2020](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2000020075). The prior review recorded a 12 July 2026 last-change status for Resolution 231 and 24 December 2024 for Resolution 14. This implementation pass does not independently certify the current legal revision.

Resolution 14 includes temporary wording through 31 December 2026 and replacement wording from 1 January 2027. Do not mix future wording into October 2026 checks. A lockable/closable entrance requirement is not a universal always-closed-door rule. Digital identification and document delivery mean absence of paper is not proof of omission. Ordinary client intake/payout is excepted from the separate supervisory-presence provision. A cup alone proves neither contents nor ownership nor prohibition.

All descriptions below paraphrase the previously reviewed public clauses. The JSON is the machine-readable catalog; automatic findings still require separate approved rule predicates and source-linked evidence. Pipeline 2.3 changes confidence validation, coverage handling, prompts and screening image detail. Its 195 passing Python tests and synthetic browser regression establish technical behavior. Two completed real reruns produced 4 fractional score changes each and 0 confirmed findings, taking 33.313 and 206.438 seconds. These runs demonstrate score updates and timing on those inputs, not detection accuracy or calibration. Historical 2.1 and 2.2 evaluation is retained separately.
